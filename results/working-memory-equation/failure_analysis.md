# Why the equation fell short

**The equation did not carry its development performance over to new subjects.** It matched 49.2% of development-check scores, but only 31.2% of the final-test scores. Its average error rose from 0.587 to 0.966 score units. Training agreement was 84.8%.

The final test covered 24 passages, 12 subject families, and 107 reading steps. Error rates give each family equal weight. A simple equation using length had slightly lower average error, 0.931, although our equation matched more scores exactly: 31.2% versus 26.4%. The uncertainty range for the difference includes zero, so this test does not establish that either has lower average error overall.

**The decline appeared across all four domains.** Final average error was 0.870 in science, 1.000 in history, 0.905 in practical instructions, and 1.091 in everyday relationships. Each domain contains only three test families.

**These passages were not dramatically different on the basic measurements we checked.** The following are ordinary averages across reading steps:

| Measurement | Training | Development check | Final test |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Jev-based score | 2.87 | 2.92 | 2.85 |
| Words in a step | 9.34 | 9.70 | 9.79 |
| Locally counted concept candidates | 2.85 | 3.03 | 3.10 |

Every test concept-candidate count fell within the training range. Only one test step exceeded the training word-count range. Their means and ranges changed little; differences in meaning or structure remain possible.

**Reference inconsistency does not explain the result by itself.** Jev repeats disagreed on 25.7% of training steps, 34.9% of development-check steps, and 30.8% of test steps. On the 74 test steps where all three repeats agreed, equation error was still 0.971 units.

Three stable-reference errors illustrate the remaining problem:

- “Explain how the wheel changes the pull's direction.” Jev-based score **5**; equation **2**.
- “Arrange paper tiles around the edge of a cardboard square, alternating light and dark colors.” Jev-based score **2**; equation **5**.
- “and Taylor's sash lies inside a box below both items.” Jev-based score **5**; equation **3**.

**Hypothesis:** counting surface properties misses which ideas can be grouped together and which relationships must remain separate. The large training-to-test gap also suggests the selected equation learned patterns too specific to this small development collection. These are explanations to investigate, not established causes.

Checks found no mismatch in frozen model/feature fingerprints, split assignments, passage offsets, repeated-score medians, or label coverage. No further equations were fitted or tested during this diagnosis.

This result rejects this equation as an accurate substitute today. It does not show that finding a better equation is impossible, and matching Jev would still not validate human working-memory measurement.
